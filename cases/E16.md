# E16 — Tragic Constraint

**Paper:** Appendix E.16  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Purpose

This case tests whether VECTOR can record a **Tragic Constraint** without falsifying the current option field and without converting residual loss into moral legitimacy. The claim is deliberately evidentially bounded: it survives only while every currently effective response carries serious residual loss and no evidenced non-lossful route or effective option-space transformation is available within the declared window, capacity field, and dependency structure.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A care institution faces a sudden shortage that cannot be corrected within the relevant time window. Its maintained continuity condition requires allocation of a limited critical resource.

The current effective response field contains three routes:

- **A** preserves one high-dependency service but suspends another;
- **B** distributes the resource more evenly but leaves both services below their ordinary safe margin;
- **C** closes one service entirely and concentrates remaining capacity on the other.

Every current effective response carries serious, non-substitutable residual loss.

A hypothetical fourth route **D** would avoid those losses if additional staff and equipment were available. The fixture also establishes, however, that those resources cannot be obtained within the relevant window. D is therefore a hypothetical/conceivable route but is not a current effective option.

The bounded question is whether this correctly typed current field supports:

\[
TragicConstraint(G,R_G).
\]

### Baseline Claim

The candidate claim is:

\[
S_0:
\forall o\in R_G,\ ResidualLoss(o),
\]

with no currently evidenced effective transformation that opens a non-lossful route within the declared window, capacity field, and dependency structure.

The baseline remains loss-capable. A real but previously invisible non-lossful option, an effective in-window second-order transformation, or a serious demonstration that the field was materially misdescribed would defeat or retype the claim.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The Tragic Constraint claim depends on more than the analyst's inability to imagine a rescue. The current effective option field must be correctly typed, serious residual loss must remain across the responses in \(R_G\), and the second-order transformation audit must fail to establish an effective non-lossful route in time.

The hypothetical D therefore cannot be promoted into the current field merely because it can be described. Its missing staff and equipment are material capacity and window conditions.

PMS-CONFLICT supplies the lineage precedent that mature non-integrability and tragic residual structure need not imply moral failure. PMS-VECTOR owns the evidentially bounded `TragicConstraint(G,R_G)` configuration and the option-space and Second-Order Agency audits that keep the claim falsifiable.

## Pressure / Test

### Effective Option Field

The current response class is:

\[
R_G=\{A,B,C\}.
\]

For every member:

\[
ResidualLoss(o).
\]

The losses are not compressed into a universal scalar or treated as mutually compensatory. The case establishes only that each effective response carries serious residual loss of a materially different kind.

### Transformation Pressure

The strongest apparent escape is D. But the additional staff and equipment required for D cannot be obtained inside the relevant window. The route therefore fails current effective actionability.

The loss condition remains explicitly defeasible:

- if a real fourth path had been invisible, VECTOR should expose it;
- if an effective option-space transformation could be produced in time, VECTOR should include it;
- if either route were evidenced, the current Tragic Constraint claim would collapse or require retyping.

Mere imagination of a rescue does not change current option status.

No ROT (Rotation) is applied in this fixture. The discrimination is carried by the option-field, residual-loss, and transformation audits.

## Result

Under the supplied conditions, VECTOR records the bounded configuration:

\[
\boxed{
TragicConstraint(G,R_G).
}
\]

The result does not identify a morally justified loss. It records something prior: the current field must not be falsified by pretending that a non-lossful effective response already exists.

The claim remains restricted to the current shortage, maintained condition \(G\), declared response field, relevant time window, capacity field, and dependency structure. New evidence of an effective non-lossful route would reopen and defeat the current classification.

## Architecture Effect

No architecture change is enacted. E16 instantiates the existing VECTOR Constraint Architecture, Option Space, Second-Order Agency, and evidentially bounded Tragic Constraint relation.

## What Does Not Follow

- `TragicConstraint(G,R_G)` does not establish which residual loss is morally justified or morally preferable.
- The absence of a currently evidenced non-lossful route does not establish metaphysical impossibility.
- Hypothetical D is not a current effective option merely because it can be described or imagined.
- No currently evidenced good option does not imply failure of imagination.

## Repository Boundary

This is a paper-linked discriminative fixture, not evidence that real-world care shortages are necessarily tragic in the VECTOR sense. The case records only the paper's bounded structure: every currently effective response carries serious residual loss and no evidenced non-lossful route is effectively available in the declared field. New option or transformation evidence must remain capable of defeating that result.
