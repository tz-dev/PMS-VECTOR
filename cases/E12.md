# E12 — Distributed Agency

**Paper:** Appendix E.12  
**Format:** Micro-Countercase  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Tempting Misclassification

No individual department can authorize, fund, and implement a needed change. An individual-only actor model therefore concludes:

\[
Capacity(a_i)<Requirement\quad\forall i
\]

and then promotes that individual insufficiency into the stronger claim:

\[
NoEffectiveTransformation.
\]

That second move is the error under test. It excludes the needed transformation from the relevant effective / transformably reachable field solely because no single department can carry it.

## Minimal Countercase

For each individual department:

\[
Capacity(a_i)<Requirement.
\]

But the coordinated configuration of three departments satisfies:

\[
Capacity(a_1+a_2+a_3)\ge Requirement.
\]

The relevant carrier of the transformation is therefore the **distributed configuration**, not any individual department taken alone. The individual-only reconstruction has excluded a transformation that the fixture directly establishes as available at the coordinated actor configuration.

PMS-LOGIC supplies lineage for effective, frame-bound capacity rather than abstract possibility. PMS-VECTOR owns the further Second-Order Agency typing and the option-field discrimination applied here.

## Correct VECTOR Result

The record result is **Revision Required**. The individual-only reconstruction exhibits **Option Blindness** through distributed-agency erasure:

\[
\boxed{DistributedAgencyErasure\rightarrow OptionBlindness}.
\]

The error is an unsupported exclusion, not mere Option Invisibility. The distributed configuration need not first be psychologically invisible; the error occurs because the reconstruction treats individual incapacity as evidence that no effective transformation is available.

The corrected capacity relation is:

\[
\boxed{distributed\ capacity\neq equal\ individual\ capacity}
\]

and the downstream attribution firewall remains separate:

\[
\boxed{collective\ capacity\neq equal\ individual\ responsibility}.
\]

The case does not need to invent a sovereign collective actor. It only needs to preserve the configuration at which the effective capacity relation is actually satisfied.

## What Does Not Follow

- The fact that the three departments together can produce the change does not mean that any one department can do so alone.
- Option Blindness in the individual-only reconstruction does not imply that the historical actors themselves failed to see the route or were responsible for the analytic exclusion.
- Collective transformative capacity does not imply equal or undifferentiated responsibility for each department.
- Distributed capacity in this fixture does not establish that every apparently blocked transformation is collectively reachable.

## Repository Boundary

E12 is a paper-linked discriminative fixture for distributed Second-Order Agency, effective-capacity typing, and Option Blindness as unsupported exclusion through distributed-agency erasure. It preserves the distinction between configuration-level capacity, individual capacity, and downstream responsibility attribution. It does not create a general theory of collective responsibility or modify the PMS-LOGIC baseline.
