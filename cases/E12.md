# E12 — Distributed Agency

**Paper:** Appendix E.12  
**Format:** Micro-Countercase  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Tempting Misclassification

No individual department can authorize, fund, and implement a needed change. A simplistic actor model could therefore conclude that effective transformative capacity is absent, or it could treat the later collective capacity as though every department possessed the same capacity and responsibility.

Both moves are blocked.

## Minimal Countercase

For each individual department:

\[
Capacity(a_i)<Requirement.
\]

But the coordinated configuration of three departments satisfies:

\[
Capacity(a_1+a_2+a_3)\ge Requirement.
\]

The relevant actor for the transformation is therefore the **distributed configuration**, not any individual department taken alone.

PMS-LOGIC supplies lineage for effective, frame-bound capacity rather than abstract possibility. PMS-VECTOR owns the further Second-Order Agency typing that allows effective transformability to reside in a coordinated actor configuration.

## Correct VECTOR Result

The record result is **Bounded Orientation**: the field contains distributed Second-Order Agency for the needed change.

The classification preserves both sides of the fixture:

\[
\boxed{distributed\ capacity\neq equal\ individual\ capacity}
\]

and:

\[
\boxed{collective\ capacity\neq equal\ individual\ responsibility}.
\]

The case does not need to invent a sovereign collective actor. It only needs to preserve the configuration at which the effective capacity relation is actually satisfied.

## What Does Not Follow

- The fact that the three departments together can produce the change does not mean that any one department can do so alone.
- Collective transformative capacity does not imply equal or undifferentiated responsibility for each department.

## Repository Boundary

E12 is a paper-linked discriminative fixture for distributed Second-Order Agency and effective-capacity typing. It preserves the distinction between configuration-level capacity and individual attribution. It does not create a general theory of collective responsibility or modify the PMS-LOGIC baseline.
