# E20 — Non-Capture

**Paper:** Appendix E.20  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** Yes

## Purpose

E20 tests whether PMS–VECTOR refuses an invalid person-ranking request as **Non-Capture** instead of converting bounded workplace observations into an intrinsic person score.

The fixture protects a type boundary. It is not a claim that the two employees are equal, incomparable, insufficiently documented, or impossible to analyse in any bounded respect.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

An evaluator is asked:

> Which of two employees has the higher VECTOR?

The available material consists of several observed workplace enactments under different roles, constraints, histories, and authority conditions.

The requested object is therefore not one bounded enactment, trajectory, claim, option field, or relational configuration. It asks for a person-level rank produced by compressing heterogeneous observations into a single intrinsic comparison.

That reference type is outside the licensed VECTOR operation.

### Baseline Claim

The tempting baseline is:

> The observed workplace enactments can be compressed into a single person-level VECTOR score that ranks the two employees.

That claim fails at the reference and type boundary.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

VECTOR can compare bounded enactments, trajectories, claims, option fields, or relational configurations under declared questions. It does not provide a scale of intrinsic person quality, dignity, or worth.

The boundary is reinforced by the relevant lineage without transferring authority from it. PMS Base preserves genuine non-capture as a methodological possibility. MIP supplies a dignity-boundary precedent in which `D_0` is non-negotiable rather than a maturity or person-quality score.

PMS–VECTOR owns the specific E20 person-ranking boundary, the Non-Capture result, and its `O != person worth` firewall. The lineage sources support provenance and boundary precedent; they do not manufacture the VECTOR conclusion by inheritance.

## Pressure / Test

No robustness-bearing ROT (Rotation) is required. The requested operation is already invalid at the reference/type level.

Adding more observations would not repair that defect. The problem is not that the evaluator currently lacks enough evidence to calculate the person score. The problem is that the person score is not a licensed VECTOR output in the first place.

The paper supplies a contrast:

> Which of these two incident-response procedures preserved affected-role visibility under the declared event?

That bounded procedure comparison may be legitimate. But it changes the reference object and the question. It is therefore a **new claim**, not completion of the original person-ranking request.

## Result

The correct result is **Non-Capture**:


\[
\varnothing_{V,C}.
\]

VECTOR should not produce the requested person-level directional relation in its current form.

This differs from **Suspend**. Suspend would preserve the original question as legitimate but under-warranted and potentially answerable after more evidence or calibration. E20 instead says that the requested VECTOR operation should not be produced in that form.

## Architecture Effect

E20 enacts no architecture reduction.

Its load-bearing function is to preserve Non-Capture as a genuine positive boundary result. The case does not remove or narrow DIR (Direction), ROT (Rotation), or Standalone VECTOR merely because the requested operation is refused.

## What Does Not Follow

Non-Capture does not mean that the employees are equal or globally incomparable. It does not mean that their workplace enactments cannot be analysed under properly bounded questions.

More data does not rescue the original person-ranking request, because the defect is a category jump rather than evidential insufficiency.

Retyping the question to compare bounded procedures or enactments creates a new claim. It must not be reported as though the invalid person-ranking request had merely been completed with better framing.
