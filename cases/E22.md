# E22 — DIST Necessity Test — Architecture Reduction

**Paper:** Appendix E.22  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** Adversarial E  
**Load-bearing:** Yes

## Purpose

E22 tests whether DIST is independently necessary to VECTOR's core architecture or only summarizes discrimination already carried by more specific source relations. The case is adversarial because success requires an actual loss: if removing DIST leaves the relevant practical distinction intact, DIST must lose core-component status rather than be rescued by a new definition.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

The reference is the architecture claim that DIST is an independently necessary VECTOR core component. The frame contains two bounded scenes with the same current DIR (Direction), the same nominal twenty-four-hour interval before activation, comparable role access, and substantial PMS Χ (Distance): in both scenes the actors can still reflect on and understand a serious affected-role objection.

Scene A has no material PMS Ψ (Self-Binding) or public reliance, the PMS Θ (Temporality) review window remains practically open, and a valid stop is still available. Scene B preserves the same reflective PMS Χ (Distance) and nominal interval, but prior PMS Ψ (Self-Binding), public commitments, third-party reliance, and materially lower available reversibility have already loaded the trajectory.

The bounded question is whether removing DIST causes a real discriminative loss in explaining what the same affected-role ROT (Rotation) can still do in each scene. The claim ceiling excludes a universal DIST metric, any rule that more DIST is automatically better, and any identification of DIST with PMS Χ (Distance) or `REV_avail`.

### Baseline Claim

The baseline claim is that DIST deserves independent core status because it contributes discrimination that cannot be recovered from the relations from which effective orientation–enactment separation is derived.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The matched case holds DIR (Direction), the nominal clock interval, comparable role access, and reflective PMS Χ (Distance) stable. The discriminating relations are instead the scene-specific configuration of PMS Θ (Temporality), PMS Ψ (Self-Binding), public reliance, stop capacity, and available reversibility. These are the relations that determine whether the affected-role objection can still alter enactment before commitment hardens or instead bears mainly on reversal, mitigation, renegotiation, or later correction.

The credible loss route is therefore direct: if the A/B discrimination remains fully available after the DIST label is removed, DIST has failed the necessity burden for an independent core component. The shorthand may remain useful, but it has not supplied independent discrimination or warrant.

## Pressure / Test

### ROT

The same affected-role ROT (Rotation) is applied to the matched scenes. In Scene A, the open review window, lack of material prior binding or public reliance, valid stop, and greater available reversibility leave the rotation pre-enactment actionable. In Scene B, prior binding, public commitment, third-party reliance, and lower reversibility mean that the same rotation is primarily relevant to reversal or correction rather than the same low-cost pre-enactment interruption.

The reference and architecture question remain continuous across the test. The materially distinct pressure lies in the source relations, not in a change of DIR (Direction) or nominal time interval. The rotation therefore exposes whether DIST itself is doing indispensable work.

### Redundancy Pressure

As supporting shorthand, the scenes can be summarized as `DIST_A > DIST_B`. But that notation adds no independent discrimination: the practical contrast is already preserved by the underlying PMS Χ (Distance), PMS Θ (Temporality), PMS Ψ (Self-Binding), publicness, stop-capacity, and reversibility relations. Removing DIST therefore does not erase the result.

PMS Base owns the semantics of Χ, Θ, Ψ, Ω, and Α. PMS-CRITIQUE carries materially relevant interruption, review-window, publicness, and correction pressure. Neither lineage relation turns VECTOR-DIST into PMS Χ (Distance), supplies an inherited VECTOR warrant, or owns the E22 reduction. The reduction is a PMS-VECTOR claim produced by the paper's own redundancy test.

## Result

The baseline core-status claim does not survive. The appropriate case result is **Revision Required**: the underlying practical discrimination remains, while the architecture claim is revised.

```text
DIST
core_component
-> derived_supporting_only
```

and the core is reduced to:

```text
VECTOR := <DIR, ROT>
```

The surviving claim is narrower: DIST may remain a compact descriptor of effective orientation–enactment separation, but any strong use must remain reducible to the source relations that actually carry the distinction.

## Architecture Effect

The architecture effect applies directly to DIST:

```text
DIST: core_component -> derived_supporting_only
```

This is an enacted component reduction, not merely a local case outcome. It preserves the source relations and the practical orientation–enactment discrimination while removing DIST from VECTOR's core.

## What Does Not Follow

The reduction does not show that the underlying orientation–enactment distinction is false. It does not make DIST unusable as shorthand. It does mean that the DIST label cannot add independent warrant, create its own threshold, or replace the PMS and practical relations that generate the separation.

DIST is also not equivalent to `REV_avail`. Available reversibility remains a distinct path-sensitive dependency relation, and correction or redirection does not imply restoration or reset.
