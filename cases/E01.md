# E01 — Rotation Confirms DIR under Serious Rival Pressure

**Paper:** Appendix E.1  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** Adversarial A  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Purpose

This case is the positive robustness fixture for PMS-VECTOR. It tests whether a bounded DIR (Direction) can survive materially relevant ROT (Rotation) pressure and a serious rival that has a real route to win, without turning survival into final truth or assimilating the rival into VECTOR vocabulary.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A small organization must choose between two incident-reporting procedures after a recurring safety problem. Procedure X records only incidents that caused immediate operational failure. Procedure Y records failures and near-misses, preserves which roles were exposed, and keeps the report open to later correction when additional information arrives.

The declared question is narrow:

> Which procedure better preserves the consequence and exposure structure needed for later corrective learning without creating a claim-critical reporting burden?

The comparison is bounded to the current safety-review process and the supplied evidence pattern. It is not a universal ranking of reporting systems.

### Baseline Claim

Under the initial operations perspective, the baseline DIR (Direction) is:

\[
X\prec_{V,C}Y.
\]

The warrant is not that Y resembles preferred PMS language. It depends on the case-supplied reporting completeness, recoverability of role-specific exposure, whether additional reporting burden suppresses participation, and whether the retained information is actually used in later correction.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The baseline remains genuinely vulnerable. It would lose if the extra near-miss layer materially reduced reporting quality, hid hazards through reporting burden, ceased to support later correction, or if a simpler rival preserved the same role-specific exposure and corrective pathways more reliably.

Those operational facts are supplied by the fixture. VECTOR does not generate them by notation.

## Pressure / Test

### Affected-Role ROT

An actor / affected-role ROT (Rotation) examines the same procedures from the position of staff who bear the safety exposure. The procedures, frame, question, and comparison remain stable.

The rotation reveals near-miss exposure that X does not preserve because no immediate outage occurred. The comparison does not reverse. Y continues to preserve a relation that is material to later corrective learning.

### Temporal ROT

A temporal ROT then extends the same comparison across several review cycles. This is materially distinct pressure: it tests whether Y's additional information continues to support correction or merely accumulates as unused record volume.

Within the fixture, the additional information continues to support correction. The baseline direction therefore survives this pressure as well.

### Serious Rival

Rival procedure R remains in its own operational vocabulary. It uses a compact hazard-control record based on exposure counts, event severity, and corrective-action closure rather than preserving every near-miss narrative. R is simpler and cheaper to administer.

R also has a genuine route to win: if its aggregation preserved the same role-specific exposure and correction pathways with less reporting friction, it would defeat Y for the declared question.

In the bounded case, however, R's aggregation removes which role repeatedly bears the near-miss exposure. The review can therefore no longer distinguish a recurring transferred-risk pattern. That loss is claim-critical for the declared corrective-learning question.

R's administrative advantage remains real, but it does not compensate for the missing exposure relation.

## Result

The baseline claim survives without material revision:

\[
\boxed{RobustOrientation(Y\succ X\mid C).}
\]

The repository records **Robust Orientation** only within the declared scope. R remains a serious but locally losing rival; it is not translated into VECTOR terminology in order to make it lose.

## Architecture Effect

E01 enacts no architecture-level change. It demonstrates the standing Robust Orientation contract: a claim may earn that result only when its claim-relevant core survives materially relevant pressure, including a serious rival with a credible route to defeat it.

## What Does Not Follow

The result does not show that Y is the universally superior reporting system, that richer records are always better, or that R is generally inferior. A different question about administrative load or a different evidence pattern could reverse the comparison.

Most importantly:

\[
\boxed{
\text{survival under relevant ROT and rival pressure}
\neq
\text{final truth}.
}
\]
