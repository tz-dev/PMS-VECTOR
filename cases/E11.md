# E11 — Second-Order Agency Claim Collapses

**Paper:** Appendix E.11  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** Yes — Adversarial D  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Purpose

This case tests whether a plausible field-transformation proposal can be classified as effective Second-Order Agency merely because the analyst can describe a mechanism and identify people with the relevant skills.

The required distinction is stricter. Second-Order Agency concerns effective change to the option field or relations among options. A current claim therefore needs a traceable actor or actor configuration, a target change, a mechanism, authority or effective capacity, dependencies, a live temporal window, evidence, and a resulting node and/or relation change.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A continuity-critical service faces two unattractive current staffing responses during a short absence window. A project lead proposes a third path: temporarily move qualified staff from a neighboring unit and preserve both functions.

The proposal is not dismissed as fantasy. The neighboring unit has people with the relevant skills, and an organizational transfer mechanism exists. The bounded question is whether this proposal counts as effective Second-Order Agency that enlarges the **current** option field inside the relevant window.

The fixture also establishes the limiting conditions: the project lead can request reassignment but cannot authorize it; the authorizing role is unavailable until after the window closes; the neighboring unit cannot release staff without that authorization; no standing emergency mechanism bypasses the dependency; and no other actor configuration capable of producing the transfer in time is evidenced.

### Baseline Claim

The baseline treats the proposed temporary reassignment as a serious field-transformation candidate:

\[
R_{O,t}\rightarrow R_{O,t+1}.
\]

The stronger claim under test is that the proposal already constitutes effective Second-Order Agency and therefore opens a third current staffing option.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The proposal has enough support to be taken seriously: relevant skills exist and an organizational transfer mechanism exists. That is not yet sufficient for effective field transformation.

The claim is vulnerable at the precise points required by the Second-Order Agency mechanism burden: actor configuration, authority/effective capacity, dependency satisfaction, and temporal window. PMS-ANTICIPATION supplies a lineage boundary that effective capacity is frame- and time-bound; PMS-VECTOR owns the broader Second-Order Agency architecture and the present collapse rule. Dependency is not warrant inheritance.

## Pressure / Test

### Mechanism and Authority/Capacity Audit

The project lead can request reassignment but cannot authorize it. The role with authorization capacity will not be available until after the relevant absence window closes. The neighboring unit cannot release staff without that authorization, and there is no standing emergency mechanism that bypasses the dependency.

The case also supplies no evidence of another individual or distributed actor configuration capable of authorizing and producing the transfer in time.

The existing transfer mechanism therefore does not become an effective current transformation merely because it exists institutionally. The mechanism cannot produce the required change inside the relevant field and window.

### Option-Field Status

The proposed reassignment is **visible** and institutionally conceivable. It is not established as **effectively actionable** for the relevant actor configuration inside the current window.

This distinction matters because:

\[
visible\neq effectively\ actionable.
\]

The proposal remains useful as a special-case probe. It identifies an institutional dependency that could be redesigned for future cases. But a useful probe is not thereby a valid current option, and the analyst's ability to describe a field transformation does not itself create the authority or capacity needed to realize it.

### ROT

No ROT (Rotation) burden is material to this fixture. E11 is not a test of a robustness-bearing DIR (Direction). The decisive pressure is the mechanism, authority/effective-capacity, dependency, and temporal-window audit required by the Second-Order Agency claim itself.

## Result

The Second-Order Agency claim collapses:

\[
\boxed{SecondOrderAgencyClaim\rightarrow Collapse.}
\]

The appropriate record result is **Revision Required**. The cross-unit reassignment remains a serious and informative probe, but it is not a current effective transformation and does not enlarge the effective option field inside the declared absence window.

No effective node or relation change is recorded in the current option field because the required transformation is not produced in time.

## Architecture Effect

No architecture-level reduction is enacted by E11. In the final model, supporting Second-Order Agency remains retained narrowly and subject to the mechanism burden exercised by this case. The fixture demonstrates the required collapse condition; it does not itself remove or newly reduce the construct.

## What Does Not Follow

The collapse does not establish that cross-unit reassignment is impossible in every future case. A changed authorization structure, a reopened window, or evidence of a distributed actor configuration capable of producing the transfer in time could support a new transformation claim.

The result is therefore bounded to the current mechanism, capacity field, dependencies, and temporal window.

Nor does imagining an institutional redesign create present capacity inside the current window.
