# E08 — Option Illusion

**Paper:** Appendix E.8  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Purpose

This case tests whether a highly visible and superficially plausible alternative is unsupportedly included in an actor's effective option field when the relevant actor lacks the conditions required to act on it. The fixture is specifically about role-relative option status, effective capacity, and the positive error direction of **Option-Field Misrepresentation**.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A manager argues that an overloaded employee can simply delegate a recurring task to another unit. The route is easy to describe and highly visible. The bounded question is narrower: **is that delegation route effectively actionable for the employee under the current role, resource, timing, and dependency conditions?**

The supplied scene answers the relevant actionability conditions directly. The employee lacks authority to transfer the task, access to the receiving unit, budget for the transfer, an open temporal window, and the cooperation on which the transfer depends.

### Baseline Claim

The tempting baseline claim is that delegation belongs to the employee's current effective option field because it is visible and appears to be a plausible way to remove the recurring task.

That claim must remain distinct from mere legibility. A route can be visible without being effectively actionable.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The case does not leave the employee's actionability status merely Unknown. The fixture positively establishes the relevant failures of authority, access, means, timing, dependency satisfaction, and practical control.

The manager therefore makes a positive option-field error: the delegation route is **unsupportedly included** in the employee's effective option field. In VECTOR's current taxonomy:

\[
\boxed{OptionIllusion=UnsupportedInclusion.}
\]

PMS-ANTICIPATION supplies a lineage boundary that attribution depends on effective frame-bound capacity and that merely imagined or later-recognized possibility does not retroactively create responsibility. PMS-VECTOR owns the specific Option-Field Misrepresentation taxonomy, role-relative option-actionability architecture, and E08 classification; the lineage relation does not itself create VECTOR warrant or transfer capacity between roles.

## Pressure / Test

### ROT

ROT (Rotation) changes the relevant role from the overloaded employee to a senior director while preserving the same nominal transfer route and bounded actionability question. The senior director can make the transfer actionable.

That result does not rescue the employee-level claim. Instead, the role rotation exposes the error more sharply:

\[
Actionable_{director}(o)
\not\Rightarrow
Actionable_{employee}(o).
\]

The same scene can therefore contain materially different effective option fields for different roles.

### Option-Status Pressure

For the employee, the delegation route remains **visible** but fails effective actionability. The failure is not lack of imagination or awareness. It is the absence of the practical relations required to enter the route under the current conditions.

The case therefore blocks both possibilist inflation and role-transfer inflation: conceivability or visibility does not manufacture availability, and an option actionable for one role is not thereby actionable for another.

## Result

The baseline employee-level option claim requires revision. Delegation is retained as a visible and conceivable route, but its inclusion in the employee's current effective option field is unsupported. That positive misrepresentation is **Option Illusion**.

The senior director's actionability remains a valid role-relative comparison result. It is not transferred back to the employee.

A future change in authority, access, resources, cooperation, timing, or practical control could support a new option-status claim. Likewise, Second-Order Agency could become relevant if a real mechanism for changing those conditions were evidenced. The current fixture establishes no such transformation.

## Architecture Effect

No architecture change is enacted. E08 is a local discriminative fixture for **Option Illusion as unsupported inclusion within Option-Field Misrepresentation**.

## What Does Not Follow

- Conceivable or visible does not imply structurally available or effectively actionable.
- Actionable for the senior director does not imply actionable for the employee.
- The present Option Illusion does not establish permanent impossibility under changed institutional conditions.
- Option-Field Misrepresentation does not by itself establish responsibility, blame, obligation, or authority.

## Repository Boundary

This case is a paper-linked discriminative fixture. It does not empirically validate VECTOR, create a universal delegation rule, or add a new option-status taxonomy. Its function is to preserve the paper's role-relative distinction between visible possibility and effective practical capacity while instantiating the positive error direction of the already-defined option-field taxonomy.
