# E21 — Serious Rival Wins

**Paper:** Appendix E.21  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** Adversarial C  
**Load-bearing:** Yes

## Purpose

E21 tests whether PMS–VECTOR can lose a narrow specialist burden to a better calibrated domain-specific rival without assimilating that rival and without converting local deference into the stronger same-burden reduction enacted in E23.

The case is therefore a test of legitimate rival superiority, not a contest designed to make VECTOR retain primary status.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A service team must choose among staffing schedules for a measured arrival pattern. The declared question is deliberately narrow:

> Which schedule minimizes expected queue delay under the measured arrival and service distributions while meeting the fixed staffing constraint?

The frame includes the candidate staffing schedules, measured arrival rates, service-time distributions, the fixed staffing constraint, and expected queue delay as the technical target.

A VECTOR analysis can reconstruct role exposure, option availability, trade-offs, staffing constraints, and consequences. Those broader distinctions remain available, but they are not allowed to silently enlarge the declared technical burden.

### Baseline Claim

The baseline claim is that VECTOR should retain primary analytical status for the queue-delay minimization burden because it can reconstruct the surrounding staffing configuration and its broader praxeological relations.

That baseline remains exposed to a specialist rival with a credible route to win.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The rival is a calibrated domain-specific queueing model supplied with the measured arrival rates, service-time distributions, and staffing limits. It produces out-of-sample queue-delay predictions that discriminate the candidate schedules directly.

Its calibration and predictive discrimination are external domain warrant. VECTOR does not generate that calibration, and re-expression of the rival result in DIR (Direction) notation would not make the warrant endogenous to VECTOR.

The baseline is vulnerable if the specialist model performs the declared discriminative work more directly and with stronger task-specific calibration while VECTOR's broader distinctions do not improve prediction of the target variable.

## Pressure / Test

### ROT

The material ROT (Rotation) family is `rival_model`.

The test changes the primary analytical architecture while holding constant the candidate schedules, measured arrival rates, service-time distributions, fixed staffing constraint, and queue-delay objective. The rival remains in its own technical vocabulary rather than being recoded as a VECTOR model.

The credible loss route is straightforward: if the calibrated queueing model directly discriminates the schedules on the declared queue-delay burden and VECTOR's additional machinery does not improve that discrimination, VECTOR loses primary status for the task.

### Specialist Rival

The rival wins that burden. The paper expressly treats its out-of-sample queue-delay predictions as more direct and more strongly domain-calibrated for the declared question.

This is a genuine local loss. It is not repaired by pointing out that VECTOR can also expose role-specific costs or hidden options, because those relations answer broader questions than the one declared here.

## Result

The canonical record status is **Bounded Orientation**, while the paper-level discrimination is:

\[
RivalSuperiority
\rightarrow
VECTOR\ ScopeReduction.
\]

For this queue-delay minimization burden, the calibrated domain-specific queueing model should carry primary analytic status and VECTOR should yield locally. The baseline claim to primary VECTOR ownership therefore collapses into domain-task deference.

The fixture does not supply numerical queue-delay predictions or identify the selected staffing schedule. It establishes the local burden transfer; it does not reconstruct a schedule ranking that the paper does not provide.

## Architecture Effect

No architecture change is enacted by E21.

E21 does **not** narrow DIR (Direction), ROT (Rotation), or Standalone VECTOR on VECTOR's own bounded orientation burden. That stronger same-burden reduction belongs to E23.

The scope loss here is local: this is the paper's VECTOR **ScopeReduction** for the specialist queue-delay burden. VECTOR should not claim primary ownership of that task when the calibrated queueing model performs it better.

## What Does Not Follow

The queueing model's local victory does not show that it answers questions about legitimacy, burden distribution, hidden options, or practical attribution. It does not establish universal superiority over VECTOR.

Nor may VECTOR assimilate the rival as “really” a VECTOR model merely because the local superiority relation can be represented in VECTOR-compatible language.

The models answer different burdens. For the declared specialist burden, the rival wins.
