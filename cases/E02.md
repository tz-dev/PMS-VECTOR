# E02 — Rotation Collapses DIR

**Paper:** Appendix E.2  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Purpose

This case tests whether ROT (Rotation) can genuinely damage a directional claim rather than functioning as a confirmation device. The target is a bounded DIR (Direction) that initially favors one staffing arrangement because it produces smoother project-level coordination.

The case is successful as a discriminative fixture if the initial direction is allowed to collapse when a materially relevant affected-role rotation exposes a claim-critical regression.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A project lead compares two staffing arrangements. Arrangement X distributes urgent coordination tasks among several team members. Arrangement Y assigns one experienced coordinator to absorb nearly all urgent interruptions.

From the project lead's initial perspective, Arrangement Y appears superior: delivery is smoother, meeting load falls, and failures are resolved quickly. The bounded question is not merely whether delivery looks efficient. It is:

> Which arrangement supports sustainable coordination without hiding critical load?

The comparison remains limited to these arrangements, the same project, the same time interval, and this declared question. It does not establish a universal staffing rule or a person-level ranking.

### Baseline Claim

The initial DIR (Direction) is:

\[
X\prec_{V,C}Y.
\]

The claim is initially carried by the apparent system-level advantages of Arrangement Y: smoother delivery, lower meeting load, and rapid failure resolution.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The baseline contains a direct vulnerability. The declared question explicitly includes both sustainable coordination and visibility of critical load. A result based only on system-level smoothness therefore cannot remain adequate if the smoothness depends on material load being shifted into one role and hidden from the initial comparison.

The relevant loss route is not a generic objection to specialization. It is specific: if Arrangement Y's apparent efficiency is produced by continuous unrecorded interruption, after-hours work, and loss of the coordinator's own project capacity, then the original comparison has omitted relations that are claim-critical under its own question.

## Pressure / Test

### ROT

An actor / affected-role ROT (Rotation) moves from the project lead's perspective to the coordinator's position while preserving the arrangements, project, time interval, and bounded question.

The rotation reveals that Arrangement Y's apparent efficiency depends on continuous unrecorded interruption, after-hours work, and loss of the coordinator's own project capacity. The cost was not absent from the scene; it had been externalized into one role.

The rotation therefore has a credible loss route. It does not merely add context. It changes whether the original directional claim can still be warranted.

### Non-Compensation

The observed delivery gain does not compensate for the exposed regression. This is not because VECTOR applies a universal anti-efficiency rule. It is because the declared question itself includes sustainable coordination and load visibility. A gain on system-level smoothness cannot silently offset failure on a claim-critical relation that the question explicitly preserves.

The non-compensation discipline has lineage precedent in PMS-STRATA, but the concrete E02 result is owned by PMS-VECTOR and carried by this fixture's declared question and pressure. Lineage is not inherited warrant.

## Result

The initial DIR (Direction) collapses:

\[
\boxed{DIR\rightarrow Collapse.}
\]

The appropriate record result is **Revision Required**. The observed smoother delivery, lower meeting load, and rapid failure resolution under Arrangement Y remain part of the scene, but they no longer warrant the original superiority relation for the declared sustainable-coordination question.

No replacement DIR is established here. A later design in which a coordinator role receives explicit capacity protection could become a new comparison candidate, but that would be a new claim with a new burden.

## Architecture Effect

No architecture-level change is enacted by E02. The case demonstrates that ROT must be capable of harming a DIR claim and that a collapse is a legitimate discriminative outcome. It does not narrow or remove DIR or ROT from the standing architecture.

## What Does Not Follow

The collapse does not establish VECTOR failure. In this case, collapse is the discriminative result.

It also does not establish that every coordinator-based staffing model is inferior, or that a modified coordinator role would fail. A modified arrangement would be a new reference object and a new claim. Revision is not survival.

Finally, the case does not authorize a universal compensatory or anti-compensatory score. The non-compensation relation is bounded to the declared question and its claim-critical load-visibility requirement.
