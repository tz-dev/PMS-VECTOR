# E06 — Missed Window under Excessive Delay / Preserved Analytical Distance

**Paper:** Appendix E.6  
**Format:** Micro-Countercase  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Tempting Misclassification

A team keeps a reversible pilot under review because it wants one more round of analysis. The tempting assumption is that continued review is the cautious option and therefore preserves practical possibility while maintaining analytical distance.

The case separates those relations. A reversible pilot can remain under analysis while a distinct practical branch closes through time.

## Minimal Countercase

The pilot can be extended only before a contractual deadline. The additional review adds little new pressure, but the team continues to wait and the deadline passes.

The extension branch therefore changes branch status:

\[
Deferred
\rightarrow
Lost.
\]

`Deferred` and `Lost` track the trajectory of the extension branch. They are branch statuses and do not replace the separate predicates used to type whether an option is conceivable, visible, structurally available, effectively actionable, or viable.

Continued review may preserve or widen derived DIST (Distance) as a summary of orientation–enactment separation. It does not preserve the contractual extension window. Likewise, the pilot's reversibility does not imply that every associated option remains reversible or available indefinitely.

No additional ROT (Rotation) is needed to reach this fixture's timing result: the paper directly supplies the low marginal value of the extra review and the deadline-driven branch loss. That limited closure does not waive ROT for any stronger robustness claim outside the fixture.

## Correct VECTOR Result

The baseline timing claim requires revision. Continued delay is not equivalent to preservation:

\[
\boxed{delay \neq preservation.}
\]

And wider derived DIST does not create an adequacy increase:

\[
\boxed{\text{wider derived DIST} \not\Rightarrow adequacy\uparrow.}
\]

The correct record is that analytical distance remained while the formerly available extension branch moved from **Deferred** to **Lost** when its contractual window closed. `Lost` is recorded as branch status; the post-deadline fixture does not invent a new positive option-status predicate for the closed branch.

## What Does Not Follow

- Delay does not by itself preserve a practical option.
- Wider derived DIST does not imply higher adequacy.
- Reversibility of the pilot does not imply preservation of the separate extension window.
- A Lost branch does not retroactively establish fault, earlier awareness, or earlier effective capacity beyond what the fixture supplies.

## Repository Boundary

E06 is a paper-linked timing and branch-status fixture. It does not validate VECTOR, produce a universal optimum between haste and delay, or turn derived DIST into an independent timing metric. Its function is to make explicit that analytical separation, option-status predicates, branch trajectory, and practical option preservation are different relations when temporal windows can close.
