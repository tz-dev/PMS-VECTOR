# E23 — Same-Burden Core Rival — Calibrated MCDA Reduces DIR–ROT Scope

**Paper:** Appendix E.23  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** Adversarial F  
**Load-bearing:** Yes

## Purpose

E23 tests the strongest current rival burden in PMS–VECTOR: whether a non-PMS model can address the **same bounded orientation question** and carry the declared discrimination more directly than DIR (Direction)–ROT (Rotation). Unlike E21's specialist-domain deference, E23 is not a case in which VECTOR simply yields a technical subtask it should never have owned. The rival is tested on VECTOR's own bounded orientation burden, so a rival victory can narrow DIR, ROT, and standalone VECTOR scope.

The fixture must permit genuine loss. It therefore preserves the externally supplied calibration rather than forcing the rival into VECTOR vocabulary or asking whether it agrees with PMS.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A public service must choose between two technically feasible intake procedures, A and B, for a six-month pilot. The declared frame includes both the operating team and affected service users. The supplied external evidence also includes affected-role feedback; no additional score is inferred from that feedback here. The supplied external decision record contains four criteria, normalized for this case to a common 0–100 performance scale where higher is better:

| Procedure | Processing efficiency | Error reduction | Accessibility | Reversibility |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | 95 | 65 | 55 | 50 |
| B | 80 | 85 | 90 | 90 |

VECTOR generates none of these criterion values.

The declared bounded question is:

> Which procedure is better supported for the pilot under the declared operational and affected-role criteria, while preserving the externally elicited priority range attached to those criteria?

For this fixture only, the four criteria are declared commensurable. Their relative priorities are externally supplied through:

\[
w(\lambda)=
\bigl(
0.55-0.25\lambda,
0.15+0.05\lambda,
0.15+0.10\lambda,
0.15+0.10\lambda
\bigr),
\qquad
\lambda\in[0,1].
\]

The range moves from stronger operations emphasis at \(\lambda=0\) toward stronger error-reduction, accessibility, and reversibility emphasis at \(\lambda=1\). VECTOR does not validate the elicitation merely because it is supplied. The case assumes its external warrant so that the rival can be tested on the same burden.

### Baseline Claim

The initial operations-centered perspective favors Procedure A because A has the largest processing-efficiency value. The baseline DIR (Direction) is therefore:

\[
B\prec_{V,C_{\mathrm{ops}}}A.
\]

That baseline remains visible because it must be allowed to lose.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

Affected-role pressure, including the supplied reversibility-sensitive relation, reveals that the operations-centered DIR (Direction) is not sufficient for the declared question. Procedure B is better on error reduction, accessibility, and the case-specific reversibility criterion. The initial A-oriented relation therefore has a real vulnerability.

DIR–ROT alone, however, does not generate the substantive calibration needed to determine how much the four declared criteria should count relative to one another. Without importing the supplied external calibration, the strongest responsible VECTOR output would be Trade-Off or suspension of a broader carried DIR.

This is the Calibration Boundary doing its intended work. The stronger E23 question begins only after calibration has been supplied externally: does VECTOR still add primary standalone discrimination once another architecture already carries the same calibrated orientation problem?

The rival can lose as well. Its superiority depends on supported criterion values, a genuinely warranted priority range, adequate representation of claim-critical affected roles, and responsible commensurability. If those conditions fail, the rival's aggregation loses the burden it claims to carry.

## Pressure / Test

### Affected-Role ROT with Reversibility-Sensitive Pressure

An affected-role ROT (Rotation) keeps Procedures A and B, the pilot question, the supplied performance values, and the admitted priority range fixed while changing the structural position from which the comparison is examined. Procedure B's advantages on accessibility and error reduction, together with the supplied reversibility-sensitive relation (90 for B and 50 for A on the case-specific external criterion), create material counterpressure against the initial efficiency-centered relation.

The ROT (Rotation) therefore identifies why the baseline is vulnerable. It does not manufacture the external priority calibration required to aggregate the four criteria into a broader carried direction, and the case-specific reversibility criterion is not retyped as VECTOR `REV_avail` or as a separate formal ROT family.

### Same-Burden Rival, Calibration, and Sensitivity

The rival is a **calibrated weighted-sum multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA) with one-parameter sensitivity analysis**. It remains in its own vocabulary of alternatives, normalized criterion performance, weights, aggregate scores, and sensitivity. It does not translate stakeholder positions into ROT (Rotation), does not use PMS operators, and does not require DIR (Direction) as an intermediate relation.

It evaluates:

\[
S(o\mid\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}w_i(\lambda)c_i(o).
\]

At the operations-heavy edge of the admitted range:

\[
S(A\mid0)=77.75,
\qquad
S(B\mid0)=83.75.
\]

At the affected-role/reversibility-heavy edge:

\[
S(A\mid1)=67.75,
\qquad
S(B\mid1)=86.00.
\]

Because the score difference is linear in \(\lambda\) and B is ahead at both endpoints:

\[
\boxed{
S(B\mid\lambda)>S(A\mid\lambda)
\quad
\forall\lambda\in[0,1].
}
\]

Extending the declared trade-off path outside the admitted range makes the reversal condition visible. The ordering switches only at approximately:

\[
\lambda^*\approx-0.49,
\]

which lies outside the externally admitted range and would require substantially greater processing-efficiency priority than that range permits.

The shared comparison criterion is not agreement with PMS. It is whether an analysis answers the same bounded orientation question with the supplied calibration, preserves the declared priority variation, and exposes the reversal condition with less unsupported machinery. Under that criterion, the MCDA rival wins.

The rival would not earn this result if a claim-critical affected role were omitted, the normalized scores lacked external support, the weight path were invented or chosen to protect one result, or a material relation could not responsibly be represented on the commensurable scale.

## Result

The initial operations-centered DIR (Direction) does not survive:

\[
\boxed{
B\prec_{V,C_{\mathrm{ops}}}A
\rightarrow
Collapse.
}
\]

The carried result for the declared calibrated question is instead:

\[
\boxed{
A\prec_{V,Q}B.
}
\]

The typed case result is **Bounded Orientation**. VECTOR may record the externally calibrated relation as a bounded DIR (Direction), but only while preserving the rival calibration as its warrant source. Reusing DIR notation does not turn externally elicited priorities into endogenous VECTOR warrant.

The local loss is therefore real without being converted into a universal theory defeat: A is preferred only under the initial operations-centered relation; B carries the supplied calibrated question across the full admitted sensitivity interval.

## Architecture Effect

E23 enacts scope reduction beyond the local result:

```text
DIR: core -> retain_scope_narrowed
ROT: core -> retain_scope_narrowed
StandaloneVECTOR:
  standalone_theory_candidate -> provisional_retain_scope_reduced
```

DIR (Direction) narrows because in genuinely commensurable, externally calibrated multi-criteria problems the carried ranking may be generated more directly by the rival decision model. VECTOR can reuse that ranking only as externally warranted input, not as evidence that DIR supplied the calibration.

ROT (Rotation) narrows because externally calibrated sensitivity analysis may already represent the relevant priority variation. ROT retains primary additional value only where it exposes a relation the rival omits or mistypes, such as a claim-critical affected role, false commensurability, option illusion, or consequence outside the supplied criterion set.

Standalone VECTOR becomes provisional and scope-reduced because the rival supplies the same bounded directional and perspectival discrimination more directly in this class. This is a same-burden loss, not E21-style specialist-domain deference.

## What Does Not Follow

The case does not show that MCDA replaces VECTOR across non-commensurable, poorly calibrated, category-sensitive, or Non-Capture cases. It does not show that every perspective can be represented as a weight. It does not convert a decision score into moral authority, and it does not establish the legitimacy of the externally supplied priority range.

VECTOR may still test whether the criterion set omits a claim-critical role, whether the calibration lacks external warrant, whether a non-compensatory relation has been falsely made commensurable, whether the option field contains an illusion, whether a relevant consequence falls outside the decision model, or whether a later practical-attribution question exceeds the MCDA burden. Those are different questions. They do not restore primary DIR–ROT discrimination for the bounded question the rival has already carried better.
