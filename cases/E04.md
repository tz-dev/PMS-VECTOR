# E04 — Trade-Off

**Paper:** Appendix E.4  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** Yes — explicitly included in the paper adversarial subset; no lettered paper label  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Purpose

E04 tests whether VECTOR preserves opposed warranted local relations as a Trade-Off instead of forcing them into a total order or flattening them into generic incomparability.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A public organization is choosing how to publish a preliminary technical finding.

- Version X publishes immediately with broad public reach but limited contextual explanation.
- Version Y delays publication briefly to add uncertainty ranges, affected-role context, and a correction channel.

Under the question of **speed of public warning** \(Q_1\):

\[
Y\prec_{Q_1}X.
\]

Under the question of **reversible and context-preserving communication** \(Q_2\):

\[
X\prec_{Q_2}Y.
\]

Both questions remain relevant. Delay can reduce warning value if it becomes excessive, while immediate broad publication can create binding, reputational fixation, and exposure before the finding is sufficiently contextualized.

### Baseline Claim

The tempting total-order claim is that the two relevant relations can be collapsed into one overall dominance ranking between X and Y.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

DIR (Direction) is warranted locally in opposite directions: X carries \(Q_1\) better, while Y carries \(Q_2\) better. The vulnerability lies in treating either local relation as automatically compensating for the other.

The fixture supplies no justified cross-question priority or conversion rule. VECTOR therefore cannot manufacture a weighted total merely because a single overall answer would be convenient.

## Pressure / Test

No additional ROT (Rotation) is needed to reach this fixture's declared Trade-Off result. The pressure is already carried by the opposed question-specific relations and the absence of a legitimate non-compensatory collapse rule; no robustness claim follows from that closure.

The calibration boundary is material: an overall total order would require an independently warranted rule for prioritizing or converting \(Q_1\) and \(Q_2\). None is supplied by the case.

## Result

\[
\boxed{TradeOff_V(X,Y\mid C).}
\]

**Multidirectional / Trade-Off.**

The directions are known. What is unavailable is a warranted overall dominance relation.

This is more structured than Incomparable: VECTOR can state exactly where X carries one relevant relation better and where Y carries the other better.

## Architecture Effect

E04 enacts no architecture-level reduction or scope change. It instantiates the existing distinction between opposed local directions and a legitimate non-compensatory resultant.

## What Does Not Follow

- Trade-Off does not imply Incomparable.
- Opposed local directions do not license an unsupported weighted total.
- Dominance on one relevant question does not imply overall dominance.
- A universal scale would not increase precision without a justified conversion rule.

## Repository Boundary

E04 is an adversarial paper-linked fixture, not empirical validation. Its correct outcome is the preservation of the opposed directional structure without inventing a total order.
