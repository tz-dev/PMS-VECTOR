# E10 — Real Option-Space Transformation

**Paper:** Appendix E.10  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Purpose

This case tests whether Second-Order Agency is genuinely discriminative when an actor configuration does more than select among fixed options. The key burden is practical: a real mechanism with effective authority must alter the option field or the relations among its existing options.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A technical migration initially contains two long-term options:

\[
A=\text{keep legacy system},
\qquad
B=\text{move to new system}.
\]

Under the current data interface:

\[
A\ excludes\ B.
\]

The systems cannot run simultaneously. A team with real technical authority then builds a temporary compatibility layer. The layer does not create a third long-term destination. Instead, it permits the organization to run \(A\) during staged preparation and migrate later to \(B\).

The bounded question is whether this implemented relational change counts as genuine Second-Order Agency.

### Baseline Claim

The claim under test is:

\[
R_{O,t}\rightarrow R_{O,t+1}
\]

through an effective change from:

\[
A\ excludes\ B
\]

to:

\[
A\ precedes\ B.
\]

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The case does not rely on the analyst merely imagining a cleaner migration field. The fixture supplies the mechanism and the praxis conditions: the team has real technical authority and actually builds the temporary compatibility layer.

The claim would fail if the layer existed only as an analytic design or if effective implementation capacity were absent. The boundary is therefore the standard Second-Order Agency burden:

\[
conceivable\ field\ transformation
\neq
\text{effective second-order agency}.
\]

## Pressure / Test

### Mechanism and Authority/Capacity Audit

The actor configuration is explicit: the technically empowered migration team. The target change is relational rather than nodal. The mechanism is the temporary compatibility layer. Authority/capacity is positively supplied, and the implementation occurs rather than remaining hypothetical.

The resulting field therefore preserves the same relevant option nodes while changing their relation:

\[
N_t=\{A,B\},
\]

while:

\[
R_{O,t}\neq R_{O,t+1}.
\]

More specifically:

\[
A\ excludes\ B
\rightarrow
A\ precedes\ B.
\]

### ROT

No robustness-bearing ROT (Rotation) claim is at issue. The discriminative test is whether the mechanism burden for real option-space transformation is satisfied.

## Result

The record result is **Bounded Orientation** with a positive Second-Order Agency claim. The compatibility layer is a real praxis transformation because effective technical authority, implementation, and the resulting relation change are all supplied by the fixture.

No new long-term option is created. The gain is relational:

\[
\boxed{R_{O,t}\rightarrow R_{O,t+1}}.
\]


## Architecture Effect

E10 enacts no architecture-level change. It is a positive discriminative fixture for the retained, supporting Second-Order Agency architecture and its mechanism burden.

## What Does Not Follow

- Relation transformation does not require a new option node.
- A hypothetical design document would not yet count as real option-space transformation.
