# E15 — `OUGHT_constraint`

**Paper:** Appendix E.15  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Purpose

This case tests whether VECTOR can reconstruct a constraint-relative practical necessity while keeping system carriage, person-level assignment, authority, and legitimacy on separate type levels. `OUGHT_constraint` names the effective response class required to maintain an operative condition; it does not convert that condition into a moral command.

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

A service provider maintains the explicit continuity condition:

\[
G=\text{critical service remains available during a regional outage}.
\]

Before the outage, several response paths can satisfy \(G\). Simultaneous equipment failure and loss of external supply then narrow the effective field.

Two responses still satisfy the condition:

- \(X\): use an emergency backup system;
- \(Y\): temporarily reduce service to a protected minimum.

Routine operation \(Z\) no longer satisfies \(G\).

The bounded question is therefore: **while \(G\) remains operative under the declared post-failure conditions, which effective response class still satisfies it, and what constraint-relative practical necessity follows?**

### Baseline Claim

The baseline is simply the pre-outage field: several response paths can satisfy \(G\). The fixture does not assume that this field survives the outage. Simultaneous equipment failure and loss of external supply materially change the scene, so the post-failure response class must be reconstructed rather than inherited from the earlier state.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The primary post-failure response class is:

\[
R_G=\{X,Y\}.
\]

Both members satisfy the declared \(G\)-condition, while \(Z\) does not. The fixture therefore supports a non-empty response class without requiring VECTOR to select between the surviving responses.

PMS-CONFLICT supplies a lineage precedent that infrastructure-coupled continuity can make carriage non-optional at system level while explicitly refusing the inference that a particular person morally ought to carry the load. PMS-VECTOR owns `OUGHT_constraint` as a general claim type, the effective response-class reconstruction, and the constraint-assignment and source/legitimacy boundaries applied here.

No ROT (Rotation) is applied in this fixture. The discrimination is carried by the changed effective response field and by keeping the constraint, its response class, and its normative status separately typed.

## Pressure / Test

### Constraint and Response Class

With \(G\) still operative, the primary scene supports:

\[
\boxed{
OUGHT_{constraint}(G,\{X,Y\}).
}
\]

This does not yet choose between \(X\) and \(Y\).

The paper then supplies a later failure variant. If that failure removes \(Y\), the response class becomes:

\[
R_G=\{X\}.
\]

Under that later field, \(X\) is practically necessary **for maintaining \(G\)**. The necessity remains conditional on the continued operation of \(G\).

### Revision Variant

The institution can instead abandon or revise \(G\), including by suspending the service entirely. This is a real transformation route rather than a forbidden escape.

But revision creates a new praxis situation. The fixture explicitly preserves a changed consequence topology including service loss, downstream exposure, legal or institutional consequences, and possible reversal costs. The possible reversal cost remains a consequence-topology possibility in this fixture; no separate VECTOR reversibility relation or reversal-cost magnitude is established. Exact magnitudes and bearer distributions are not invented.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
Revision(G)\neq costless\ escape.
}
\]

Revision of \(G\) therefore yields a **new constraint claim** rather than erasing the trajectory that existed while \(G\) was maintained.

## Result

For the primary post-failure scene, VECTOR records the bounded constraint relation:

\[
OUGHT_{constraint}(G,\{X,Y\}).
\]

For the later singleton variant, \(X\) becomes practically necessary for maintaining \(G\). Neither result creates moral authority. The practical relation is conditional, frame-bound, and dependent on the correctly typed effective response field.

The case also leaves person-level burden assignment open. No specific individual is made the bearer merely because the system contains a continuity requirement.

## Architecture Effect

No architecture change is enacted. E15 instantiates the existing VECTOR Constraint Architecture and `OUGHT_constraint` relation.

## What Does Not Follow

- Required for \(G\) does not mean morally required.
- An operative \(G\) does not establish the legitimacy, justification, or moral standing of \(G\).
- `OUGHT_constraint(G,{X,Y})` does not select between \(X\) and \(Y\).
- System-level carriage necessity does not imply that a particular person must carry the load.
- Revision or abandonment of \(G\) is not a costless exit from praxis.

## Repository Boundary

This case is a paper-linked discriminative fixture. It does not validate the legitimacy of the continuity condition, create legal or moral authority, or add a new Ought type. Its function is to preserve the paper's distinction between an operative constraint-relative response requirement and the separate questions of bearer assignment, authority, legitimacy, and revision consequences.
