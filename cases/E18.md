# E18 — Revision Is Not Survival

**Paper:** Appendix E.18  
**Format:** Full Minimal Case  
**Adversarial:** No  
**Load-bearing:** No

## Purpose

This case tests whether VECTOR can improve a claim without rewriting the history of its loss. A broad DIR (Direction) may collapse, a materially narrower claim may then be formulated, and that revised claim may later earn robustness. The later success must not be reported as survival of the original baseline.

The core discipline is:

\[
\boxed{Revision\neq Survival.}
\]

## Case Setup

### Reference, Frame, and Question

The comparison concerns Procedure Y and Procedure X. The initial claim is broad: Y is said to dominate X across the relevant operating context.

A serious rival model then exposes a low-resource counter-scene. Y's advantage depends on high resource availability, and under low-resource conditions Y produces a critical regression. The declared test therefore asks first whether the broad claim survives and, after it fails, whether a narrower claim can earn robustness under newly explicit conditions.

### Baseline Claim

The fixed loss-bearing baseline is:

\[
S_0:
\text{Procedure }Y\text{ dominates }X
\text{ across the relevant operating context}.
\]

The low-resource counter-scene defeats that claim. The broad claim collapses.

## Warrant and Vulnerability

The critical vulnerability is scope. The apparent advantage of Y is conditional on high resource availability, but \(S_0\) states the relation across the relevant operating context without preserving that condition.

Once the rival exposes the low-resource regression, the original claim cannot be saved by silently adding a high-resource qualifier. A material coordinate change creates a new claim burden; it does not preserve automatic continuity with the failed baseline.

## Pressure / Test

### Serious Rival and Baseline Loss

The rival model supplies a real winning condition: under low-resource conditions, Y creates a critical regression. That is sufficient to defeat \(S_0\).

The result at this stage is not a successful robustness test. It is loss:

\[
S_0\rightarrow Collapse.
\]

### Revision

The analyst then formulates a narrower claim:

\[
S_1:
\text{Under high-resource conditions }F_H,
\ Y\text{ dominates }X\text{ for }Q.
\]

This is not a repaired wording of the same surviving claim. It is a materially revised claim whose restricted frame and question must carry their own burden.

### ROT and Further Pressure

The revised claim is then subjected to relevant role ROT (Rotation). The high-resource frame \(F_H\), the procedures, and the question \(Q\) are held fixed while the materially relevant role perspective changes. The fixture states that \(S_1\) survives this pressure.

The paper also states that \(S_1\) survives temporal pressure and the serious rival. No extra role, temporal, or empirical detail is added by the repository case beyond those declared pressures.

## Result

The correct typed result is:

\[
\boxed{RevisedRobustOrientation(S_1).}
\]

The surviving claim is therefore the narrower \(S_1\), not \(S_0\). The version lineage remains visible:

\[
S_0\rightarrow S_1.
\]

A later robust revision cannot compensate for or erase the earlier collapse. The theory improves through losing the original claim.

## Architecture Effect

E18 enacts no architecture-level reduction. It demonstrates the existing result discipline that a failed baseline may generate a better revised claim, while `Revision != Survival`.

## What Does Not Follow

- Survival of \(S_1\) does not imply survival of \(S_0\).
- Collapse of \(S_0\) does not by itself establish VECTOR failure; here the loss is the discriminative result that makes revision possible.
- Revised Robust Orientation under \(F_H\) and \(Q\) does not warrant Y's dominance under low-resource or otherwise unspecified conditions.
- Narrowing a failed claim does not create automatic claim continuity.
