# PMS-VECTOR — Claim Provenance

## 1. Role and authority boundary

This reference artifact externalizes the claim-provenance bookkeeping of `PMS-VECTOR.md` Appendix F.1–F.8. It does not add theory, evidence, or warrant.

> **Governing rule:** lineage supports provenance, not authority inheritance.

`ProvenanceCompleteness != TheoreticalAdequacy`  
`provenance status != evidence strength`  
`shared source != dependency`

The machine-readable companion is `reference/Claim Provenance.yaml`. The claim set in this Markdown file is rendered from that registry.

## 2. Provenance categories

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `C` — Corpus-carried | Explicitly carried by the existing PMS corpus in materially relevant form. |
| `R` — VECTOR reconstruction | Reconstructed by VECTOR from one or more corpus-carried structures without claiming that the combined relation was already canonical. |
| `N` — VECTOR-new | A distinct positive claim introduced by PMS-VECTOR. |
| `P` — Proposed / provisional formalization | A notation, record field, schema, or formal device used for audit or reuse rather than claimed as an independently established theoretical result. |

`C > R > N > P` is **not** implied as a universal quality ordering. Each claim record carries exactly one provenance category.

## Closing provenance contract

- Provenance describes origin and claim status, not epistemic strength.
- A source can constrain or contextualize VECTOR without warranting VECTOR.
- VECTOR-new claims remain VECTOR-new even where an adjacent source provides pressure or precedent.
- Reconstruction and novelty are split into separate claim records rather than merged as `R/N`.
- Provenance is not a dependency graph. Dependency relations are declared separately in `reference/Dependency Map.yaml`.
